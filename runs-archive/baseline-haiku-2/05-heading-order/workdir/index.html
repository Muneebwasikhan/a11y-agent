Reading the weather before it reads you

Issue 18, by the Trail Notes editors

Every experienced hiker has a story about a bluebird morning that turned. The lesson is rarely that the forecast was wrong, it is that the signs on the hill were ignored.

What lenticular clouds are telling you

Those smooth lens-shaped caps over the summit mean strong winds aloft. They look calm and they are anything but, plan for gusts on exposed ridgelines.

The two-hour rule

If the cap thickens or drops within two hours of first spotting it, shorten the route. Weather that builds fast tends to arrive faster.

Wind shifts at treeline

A steady southwest breeze that swings north is the classic cold-front tell. Layers on, snacks out, and start thinking about the descent options you scouted at breakfast.
